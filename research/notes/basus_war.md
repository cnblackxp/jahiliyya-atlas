# The War of al-Basūs — research note for the first epic

*Compiled 2026-10-19 for the `basus-war` epic. Every source below was read for this
note; what each one says is quoted or paraphrased beside it. Where they disagree with
each other or with the atlas, that is recorded, not settled.*

## What the atlas has

Thirteen events, all graded `TRAD` / `legendary`, none dated by any source:

| role | event | year (range) |
|---|---|---|
| prelude | `khazaz-day` — Yawm Khazāz | 400 (300–500) |
| core | `basus-war-begins` — Kulayb kills the she-camel, Jassās kills Kulayb | 494 (480–510) |
| core | `basus-unayza`, `basus-nahy`, `basus-dhanaib`, `basus-waridat`, `basus-hanw`, `basus-qusaybat`, `basus-quda`, `basus-naqiyya`, `basus-fusayl` — the named days | 496–530 (480–545) |
| core | `hujras-kills-jassas` | 535 (510–560) |
| core | `muhalhil-dies` | 540 (530–555) |

Figures: Kulayb, Jassās, Jalīla bint Murra, al-Basūs, al-Muhalhil, Hammām ibn Murra,
al-Ḥārith ibn ʿUbād, Bujayr. Tribes: Taghlib, Bakr.

## The two names asked about

**Jalīla (الجليلة بنت مرة).** Daughter of Murra ibn Dhuhl of Shaybān; Jassās's sister
and Kulayb's wife; a poet. After her brother killed her husband she went back to her
people; her poem answering Kulayb's sister is well known, and Amal Dunqul's modern "Lā
tuṣāliḥ" draws on her. Arabic Wikipedia gives her death as c. 540 (80 BH). Already a
figure in the atlas (`jalila`), now with sources.

**The Yemeni Tubbaʿ (التبّع اليماني).** Not in the akhbār at all. He belongs to the
*popular epic*, *Qiṣṣat* or *Sīrat al-Zīr Sālim* — a *sīra shaʿbiyya*, the genre of long
popular romances written down and performed centuries after the events (the genre term was
coined by folklorists in the 1950s; some of the Zīr Sālim verse is Ottoman-period). There:

- the Yemeni king **Tubbaʿ Ḥassān** kills **Rabīʿa**, father of Kulayb and of al-Zīr;
- he is to marry al-Jalīla; her people send her trousseau in a hundred chests, each with a
  warrior hidden under a false bottom; Kulayb enters disguised as the jester *Qashmar* and
  kills the Tubbaʿ with his own sword ("you killed my father");
- al-Zīr Sālim — the sīra's name for al-Muhalhil — becomes the hero of the vengeance, and
  the sīra makes Jalīla turn Kulayb against him.

The atlas keeps this as a separate, labelled layer inside the epic ("in the popular epic,
not the akhbār"), with its own sources, and **does not** turn it into a dated event: it has
no date, and giving it one would invent precision. The nearest thing in the akhbār is
**Yawm Khazāz**, where (in one version) Kulayb leads Maʿadd against Yemen.

## Where the sources disagree

- **How long, and when.** Arabic Wikipedia: forty years from 494 for the fuller narrators,
  "some twenty" for others. English Wikipedia: over by the 530s. Ali Hussein (Ḥarb al-Basūs
  Seminar, 2023): the last battles "around the year 532". Peter Webb (same seminar): Kulayb
  belongs to the mid-sixth century — which would move the whole war decades later than the
  atlas's 494.
- **Khazāz.** Arabic Wikipedia records disagreement over who led Maʿadd (al-Aḥwaṣ ibn
  Jaʿfar, Zurāra ibn ʿUdus, or Kulayb), which Yemeni king, when and why; al-Aṣmaʿī makes it
  al-Mundhir's day with Taghlib against Kinda and Bakr — nearly the reverse. It places
  Khazāz in the sixth century, after al-Ḥārith ibn ʿAmr's death in 531. The atlas has
  `khazaz-day` at 400 (300–500). **Not changed**; the source is attached with that note.
- **al-Muhalhil's death.** Both Wikipedias: 531 (94 BH); English gives c. 443–531. Atlas:
  540 (530–555). Not changed; noted on his sources.
- **Jassās.** Both Wikipedias: died c. 534 (Munīr al-Baʿlabakkī); atlas 535. Close; noted.
- **What kind of thing it is.** English Wikipedia (after Ali Hussein, *Journal of Islamic
  Studies* 2025) and the 2023 seminar treat the war as a composite narrative that grew from a
  smaller conflict through poetry and *adab*; Webb notes that later tellings pushed Kulayb
  aside to centre the revenge, which is the direction the popular sīra takes to its limit.
